"Top 3%" is easy to claim and hard to prove. This is the actual process behind
the phrase — what we screen for, the stages a candidate moves through, and why it
produces engineers who are ready to contribute on day one.

## What "top 3%" actually means

It is not about pedigree or puzzle-solving speed. It is about the traits that
predict strong delivery on a real team over time.

### The traits that survive every market cycle

1. **Problem decomposition** — turning an ambiguous problem into shippable steps.
2. **Code judgment** — writing code the next person can safely maintain.
3. **Communication** — explaining a trade-off to a non-engineer.
4. **Ownership** — following a task to production, not just to "done on my machine".

> We reject more candidates for weak communication than for weak code.

## The four-stage evaluation

Every candidate moves through the same structured pipeline. No shortcuts, no
"they seemed nice" hires.

### Stage 1 — Signal screen

We start with evidence, not résumés.

- Real projects and contributions
- A short written exercise that reveals *how* someone thinks
- A quick check on communication and reliability

### Stage 2 — Live technical evaluation

A pair-programming session on a realistic problem.

- We watch the process, not just the final answer
- We look for how they handle ambiguity and feedback
- We probe testing and edge-case thinking

### Stage 3 — System & communication round

- An architecture discussion sized to the role
- A deliberate "explain it simply" prompt
- A look at how they reason about trade-offs

### Stage 4 — Reference & delivery check

We verify how someone actually shows up on a team over time — consistency,
follow-through, and collaboration.

## How the stages map to traits

| Stage                 | Primary signal it tests        |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Signal screen         | Evidence, communication        |
| Live technical        | Code judgment, problem-solving |
| System & communication| Architecture, clarity          |
| Reference & delivery   | Ownership, consistency         |

## What we deliberately ignore

Some popular filters are noise. We skip them on purpose.

### Brand-name employers with nothing to show

A famous logo on a résumé tells us where someone worked, not what they can do.

### Memorized algorithm puzzles

Trick questions unrelated to the work select for interview practice, not job
performance.

### Years of experience as a proxy for skill

Eight years of repeating year one is not eight years of growth. We measure the
skill directly.

## The result

A shortlist you can trust — vetted for the way real teams actually work, not for
the way interviews are usually gamed. That is what lets a Fekra engineer add value
in their first week, not their first quarter.
