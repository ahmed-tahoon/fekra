AI coding tools are now part of every serious engineering org. The question is no
longer *whether* to use them, but *how* to adopt them so they compound your team's
quality instead of quietly eroding it.

This is a practical playbook: where AI genuinely helps, where it hurts if left
unmanaged, and how to put guardrails in place that scale.

## Where AI genuinely helps

Used well, AI removes drudgery and accelerates understanding.

### Boilerplate and glue code

Scaffolding, tests, migrations, and repetitive transformations are ideal AI work —
high-volume, low-ambiguity, easy to verify.

### Exploration and onboarding

AI is excellent at helping an engineer understand an unfamiliar codebase quickly,
turning a two-day archaeology dig into an afternoon.

### Review assistance

A first pass that catches the obvious — naming, missing tests, simple bugs —
before a human spends attention on the subtle parts.

## Where it hurts if unmanaged

> AI makes it cheap to produce code and expensive to ignore review.

When teams ship AI-generated code without raising the review bar, defects and
architectural drift accumulate fast. The tooling didn't fail — the process did.

### The three failure modes

1. **Volume without verification** — more code than the team can actually review.
2. **Plausible-but-wrong** — code that looks right and fails subtly.
3. **Skill atrophy** — juniors who accept output they can't evaluate.

## A practical adoption playbook

Adopt AI like you'd adopt any powerful tool: with intent and guardrails.

### 1. Keep humans on the hook

AI drafts; engineers own. Accountability never moves to the tool.

### 2. Strengthen review, don't weaken it

More generated code means *more* scrutiny, not less. Make review the bottleneck on
purpose.

### 3. Measure outcomes, not keystrokes

Track defect rates, lead time, and change-failure rate — not "lines accepted".

### 4. Train the team

Prompting and verification are skills. Invest in them like you invest in any other
engineering capability.

## Metrics that tell the truth

| Metric              | What it reveals                     |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Change-failure rate | Is generated code actually reliable |
| Lead time           | Is the team genuinely faster        |
| Review depth        | Are humans still in the loop        |
| Defect escape rate  | Is quality holding under volume     |
